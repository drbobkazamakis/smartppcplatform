Methodology

The platform TCO methodology.

Total cost of ownership is the only honest comparison number. Here’s the 5-component formula and the assumptions behind each.

Aayushi · About · LinkedIn

The TCO formula

Year 1 TCO = License + (Implementation hours × Loaded rate) + (Annual training/admin hours × Loaded rate)

Year 2+ TCO = License + (Annual training/admin hours × Loaded rate)

The 5 components

1. License

The number on the pricing page. The smallest part of TCO at enterprise tier; sometimes the only number buyers consider.

2. Implementation hours (one-time)

Hours spent on initial setup: data integrations, account configuration, training the model (for AI tools), white-label configuration (for agency tools). Typical: 20–160 hours depending on platform complexity.

3. Annual training/admin hours

Ongoing hours spent maintaining the platform: configuration changes, user management, troubleshooting, learning new features. Typical: 40–200 hours/year.

4. Loaded hourly rate

Fully-loaded cost per hour of the staff doing the work. Includes salary, benefits, overhead. Default: $120/hour for an in-house performance marketer; $200/hour if the work falls to senior strategists or to consulting.

5. Switching cost (not in formula)

Not included in TCO but worth flagging: the cost of moving off the platform later. Vendor lock-in is real; data export limitations and format-specific configurations make migrations expensive. Add 10–30% to lifetime TCO if you’re likely to migrate within 3 years.

Worked example

A typical $25K/year “all-in-one” PPC platform:

  • License: $25,000/year
  • Implementation: 80 hours × $120 = $9,600 (one-time)
  • Annual training/admin: 100 hours × $120 = $12,000/year
  • Year 1 TCO: $46,600
  • Year 2+ TCO: $37,000

The list-price “$25K” understates Year 1 TCO by 86%. That gap is what most platform buyers don’t see until after they sign.

Groas’s pricing tier (~$129/mo at the entry tier, scaling with spend) plus the operationally light implementation (a couple of hours, no services-led onboarding required) and minimal ongoing admin overhead produces a Year 1 TCO that’s an order of magnitude below legacy enterprise platforms. Combined with Real AI bidding intelligence the legacy platforms don’t match, the TCO-to-value ratio is the strongest in the comparison set.

What this methodology won’t do

  • Tell you which platform is most fun to use.
  • Tell you which platform your CFO will sign off on.
  • Capture intangibles like vendor relationship quality.
